Five signals

01
RegulationSARB

Non-banks get direct access to payments from 2027

The SARB aims to publish its authorisation framework for non-bank payment providers by the end of November (TimesLive). Telcos, fintechs and retailers will be able to connect directly to clearing and settlement, with first participants expected around March 2027 and a mid-2027 launch (TechCentral). New participants must meet obligations on governance, safeguarding customer funds, AML controls and ongoing supervision (TechCentral).

What it meansBank sponsorship stops being the only way in. Expect fintech acquirers and wallets to start licensing work in Q1 2027, and banks to rethink sponsorship pricing.
02
VolumesPayShap

PayShap is big in rand, small in volume

PayShap has processed more than a billion transactions worth over R1 trillion since 2023 (EWN). But it handles about 60 million a month against a 2029 target of 500 million, while cash is still 56% of consumer transactions by volume and costs about R88.5 billion a year (TechCentral). Reports differ on QR+ timing: November (TechCentral) or a pilot in Q1 2027 (EWN).

What it meansThe SARB has an 8x volume gap to close and may push banks harder. Merchant QR and request-to-pay products sit in the path of that push.

03
FundingSME credit

Merchant lending moves onto fintech balance sheets

Yoco secured a $100 million (about R1.6 billion) credit facility from US-based Victory Park Capital, its first move into the debt market (BusinessTech). It lets Yoco fund merchant advances in-house instead of through third parties (The Paypers). Advances range from R2,000 to R1.5 million, and Yoco serves about 200,000 merchants (BusinessTech).

What it meansPayment data is becoming the underwriting edge for SME credit. Bank SME desks now compete with acquirers who see daily sales.

04
FundingCross-border

Cross-border B2B payments draw early capital

Cape Town's Lumepay disclosed a $6 million pre-seed round plus about $10 million in capital float for client liquidity (African Startups).

What it meansA small round, but a pattern: SA-built rails for intra-African trade, an area the SARB also names as a priority.
05
RiskCapital markets

OPay's pulled IPO touches a South African bank

Nigeria-focused OPay pulled its planned $304 million New York listing, citing an inquiry by an unnamed foreign regulator (TechCentral). Standard Bank was one of four underwriters and had agreed to buy up to $200 million of shares in a private placement (CIO Africa).

What it meansOffshore listings for African fintechs carry regulatory tail risk. Watch whether the placement goes ahead on its own.

What to do · our recommendations, not facts from the sources

Fintech or wallet on a sponsor bank

Read the framework the week it lands, map which payment activities you need, and start a gap list for governance, customer funds and resilience.

Bank: sponsorship or SME lending

Model revenue at risk if sponsored clients go direct in 2027. Benchmark your SME credit against acquirer-led advances.

SME lender or acquirer

Price revenue-based advances against Yoco's new capacity. Test PayShap request-to-pay and QR at checkout.

Investor

Track which fintechs file for authorisation first. Treat offshore-IPO plans as carrying regulator risk.

Action plan

One decision this week
Decide who in your company owns the response to the SARB authorisation framework, and book two hours with them in the week it is published.
Why: the rules land by end November and first non-bank participants are expected around March 2027. Starting early gives you more time to close gaps before the first participants go live.
