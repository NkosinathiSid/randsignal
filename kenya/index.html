The market

Kenya had about 54 million active mobile money subscriptions on 30 June 2026, more than one per person, up 13.2% in a year (Tech-ish, citing the Communications Authority). M-Pesa holds 88.8% and Airtel Money 11.1%. Its share has slipped since interoperability arrived in 2022 (TechCabal).

Digital lending is large and now licensed: 281 digital credit providers held CBK licences by end September 2026, having issued about 9.6 million loans worth KES 165 billion by August (Citizen Digital).

What it means for an SA firmAny consumer or merchant product has to work with M-Pesa from day one. The opening is in what sits around the wallets: merchant acceptance, gateways, SME credit, remittances and B2B payments.

Who regulates what

Central Bank of Kenya (CBK)

Payment service providers and system operators under the National Payment System Act 2011; also all non-deposit-taking credit providers since the Business Laws (Amendment) Act took effect on 27 Dec 2024 (Bowmans, CDH).

Data Protection Commissioner (ODPC)

Fintechs handling Kenyans' personal data must register as data controllers or processors (Chambers / CDH). Cross-border transfer guidance (8 Sep 2026) requires a Kenyan copy for some "strategic interest" processing (Covington).

What the draft NPS Bill 2026 changes

CBK and the Treasury published a draft National Payment System Bill 2026 to replace the 2011 Act. It creates 10 licence categories with minimum capital from KES 5 million to KES 250 million; public comments closed on 9 October 2026 (FintechSpecs). Firms with several licences hold the highest category's capital plus 50% for each extra one. Every provider must be interoperable and able to share customer data for open finance (same source).

Existing firms would get 12 months to comply, and no single bank could hold more than KES 500m or 25% of a firm's trust deposits, whichever is higher (HapaKenya). Bowmans reportedly reads the bill as not carrying over current licences, so holders may need to re-apply; foreign companies could apply through a registered branch (as reported by FintechSpecs).

What it meansIf you apply now, you may have to re-apply under the new categories within a year. Plan capital for the new tiers, not the old ones.

Three ways in, and who used them

1 · Partner

Tanzania's NALA entered by routing transfers through Pesalink and settling with Equity Bank, avoiding a long licence process (TechCabal). Cross Switch launched through licensed Kenyan PSP Pesawise (Zawya).
Fastest; least control.

2 · License

Nigeria's Payaza got CBK authorisation as a payment gateway on 5 Nov 2026. It covers checkout, not wallets or holding customer funds (Nairametrics).
Own the relationship; slower.

3 · Acquire

Moniepoint bought 78% of Sumac Microfinance Bank, completed by April 2026, using its licence and SME customers to launch (FF News).
Instant scale; most capital.
